## 1. Linguistic profile of the pole

High negative loadings:

- horrible (−.64)
- somehow (−.54)
- single (−.50)
- honestly (−.45)
- thinking (−.41)
- (sorry (−.39))
- clearly (−.38)
- especially (−.38)
- yet (−.36)
- (properly (−.32))

These are not random content words; they cluster into two main functional sets:

1. **Stance / evaluation / evidentiality**
   - horrible, honestly, clearly, especially, sorry, yet  
   → strong *evaluative* and *stance‑marking* items, often intensifying or framing a judgment.

2. **Cognitive / inferential / scalar framing**
   - thinking, somehow, single, properly  
   → items that:
   - highlight *mental processing* (thinking)
   - mark *unexpectedness or weak causality* (somehow)
   - pick out *individual instances* for emphasis (single)
   - mark *normative standards* (properly)

Taken together, the loadings point to a discourse that:

- foregrounds **speaker stance** and **evaluation**
- often orients to **problems, difficulties, or negative experiences**
- highlights **surprise, frustration, or incredulity** (“somehow”, “honestly”, “horrible”)
- zooms in on **specific instances** (“every single…”) to dramatise a point
- uses **metadiscursive markers** (“clearly”, “especially”, “properly”) to guide the listener’s interpretation.

## 2. What the examples show

All examples are conversational narratives or complaints with a strong evaluative overlay. The loading words are central to how the speaker positions themselves.

### a) Intensified negative evaluation of events/people

- “how horrible it is” (t003312, Twitter abuse)
- “even something as horrible as that” (t001903, extreme religious beliefs)
- “it was honestly so bad” (t001904, bad referencing)
- “that horrible cold” (t002294, mountain climb)
- “that bloke who was horrible to women” (t003303, abusive partner)
- “it’s just this horrible feeling of knowing before you even start that you’re probably gonna lose” (t002685, football)

These are not neutral descriptions; they are **emotionally loaded, judgmental accounts** of situations, often with moral or affective colouring.

### b) Explicit stance and incredulity

- “I honestly can’t imagine dealing with that kind of constant pressure” (t003312)
- “their beliefs are honestly terrifying” (t001903)
- “It was honestly so bad…” (t001904)
- “I honestly don’t get how he copes…” (t003313)

“Honestly” here is a **stance marker**: it foregrounds the speaker’s subjective reaction, often of disbelief, shock, or strong feeling.

“Somehow” similarly marks **puzzlement or weakly explained causality**:

- “he somehow still managed to get nine hundred quid for it” (t001748)
- “somehow she clung on to him anyway” (t003303)
- “I’d somehow missed three weeks of it” (t002735)
- “somehow doesn’t [get the ball]” (t002685)

These uses present events as **unexpected, hard to rationalise**, reinforcing a tone of “this really shouldn’t have happened / doesn’t make sense”.

### c) Focusing on individual instances for rhetorical effect

“Single” is almost always in **scalar emphatic** uses:

- “every single event is a decision made by God” (t001903)
- “every single reference one by one” (t001904)
- “every single thing I do with my boyfriend” (t002160)
- “we had to take every single chance we got” (t002685)

This pattern is typical of **complaints and rants**: zooming in on “every single X” to underscore how pervasive or exhausting the problem is.

### d) Metadiscourse and normativity

- “what I’ve actually done… clearly shows” (t001904)
- “my old car was so clearly on its last legs” (t001748)
- “she clearly didn’t have much self-respect” (t003303)
- “when it’s obvious he’s getting properly fed up” (t003313)

“Clearly”, “obvious”, “properly” present the speaker’s interpretation as **self‑evident** or **normatively correct**, inviting alignment and sometimes moral judgment.

“Especially” often highlights **salient, emotionally charged aspects**:

- “especially because I see his tweets pop up quite a lot” (t003312)
- “especially all that seventies stuff” (t002735)
- “especially when it’s obvious he’s getting properly fed up” (t003313)

### e) Cognitive processing and internal commentary

“Thinking” is used to report **inner commentary**:

- “it just makes you think about how intense it must be” (t003312)
- “I’m just sat there thinking, what does she actually expect?” (t002160)
- “I keep thinking, where’s it all gone then?” (t002735)
- “I’m just stood there thinking what’s the point?” (t002685)

This foregrounds **reflective, evaluative cognition** about problematic situations.

## 3. Discourse function of the pole

Putting the loadings and examples together, this pole encodes a discourse style that is:

- **Highly evaluative**: frequent explicit judgments (horrible, terrifying, awful, properly wrong).
- **Subjectively stance‑rich**: “honestly”, “clearly”, “especially”, “sorry” frame the speaker’s attitude.
- **Problem‑oriented**: many texts revolve around **complaints, frustrations, or troubling stories** (abuse, religious extremism, family conflict, poor team performance, fear on a mountain).
- **Emotionally and morally charged**: not just “bad” but “horrible”, “terrifying”, “awful”, with moral overtones (self‑respect, cruelty, unfairness).
- **Cognitively reflective**: explicit references to thinking, imagining, not understanding, puzzlement.
- **Rhetorically intensified**: “every single…”, “properly”, “honestly”, “clearly” used to dramatise and persuade.

Crucially, this is not just “negative sentiment”; it is **narrated evaluation**: speakers recount experiences or third‑party stories and actively interpret them, inviting the listener to share their stance.

The fact that GPT, especially when “profiled”, drives this pole suggests that the model, when given socio‑demographic cues, tends to **over‑produce explicit stance markers and evaluative intensifiers** to sound conversational and engaged.

## 4. Possible labels for this pole

Any label should capture:

- the **evaluative, stance‑heavy** nature of the discourse
- the focus on **problematic / troubling experiences**
- the **reflective, interpretive** commentary on those experiences.

Plausible labels:

1. **Evaluative Complaint and Problem‑Narrative Discourse**  
   - Highlights that texts are often complaints or accounts of difficulties, framed through strong evaluation.

2. **Subjective Stance and Affective Problem‑Talk**  
   - Emphasises the dense use of stance markers (“honestly”, “clearly”, “especially”) and the emotional orientation to problems.

3. **Reflective, Morally Charged Evaluation**  
   - Captures the introspective “thinking” plus the moral/affective judgments (“horrible”, “self‑respect”, unfairness).

4. **Intensified Negative Experience Framing**  
   - Focuses on how events are framed as especially bad, puzzling, or unfair, using intensifiers and scalar emphasis (“every single”, “properly”, “honestly”).

If I had to choose one primary label for this pole:

> **Intensified Evaluative Problem‑Talk**

Justification:

- **Intensified**: via “horrible”, “every single”, “properly”, “honestly”, “clearly”.
- **Evaluative**: the core loadings are stance/evaluation markers.
- **Problem‑Talk**: the examples are dominated by complaints, troubling stories, and accounts of difficulty, framed through reflection and judgment.

This label reflects both the lexical loadings (stance and intensification items) and the discourse pattern in the excerpts (narrated problems with strong subjective evaluation), and it aligns with the fact that profiled GPT is the main driver of this style.
