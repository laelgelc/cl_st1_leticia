Factor f4 (positive pole) is strongly driven by human speakers and is characterised by dense use of informal, often taboo, stance‑marking items. It encodes a discourse style that is emotionally charged, colloquial, and interpersonally involved.

Below I propose labels, then justify them from (1) loadings and (2) examples, and (3) who drives the pole.

---

## 1. Proposed labels for f4 (positive pole)

Any of the following would be defensible, depending on how compact you want the label:

1. **“Expletive‑laden colloquial stance”**
2. **“Emotionally charged, taboo‑rich conversation”**
3. **“Intensified, sweary everyday talk”**

I’ll use “expletive‑laden colloquial stance” as the primary label, because it captures:
- the central role of swearing/taboo items,
- the highly informal register,
- and the strong stance/attitude work these items are doing.

---

## 2. Evidence from factor loadings

**High positive loadings:**

- shit (1.07)
- fuck (.75)
- god (.53)
- sorry (.40)
- gonna (.39)
- guy (.38)
- like (.35)
- cos (.31)

### 2.1. Register and style

- **fuck, shit, god**: canonical expletives and taboo items, used as:
  - intensifiers (“really shit journey”, “rough as fuck”),
  - evaluative labels (“that shit is dangerous”, “this kid is fucked in the head”),
  - discourse reactions (“oh shit”, “holy fuck”, “oh my god”).
- **gonna, cos, like**: hallmark features of **informal spoken English**:
  - gonna: reduced future marker, strongly colloquial.
  - cos: reduced causal conjunction, again informal.
  - like: used as:
    - quotative (“I was like oh thank god…”),
    - approximator (“like two and a half hours”),
    - discourse filler (“so it’s difficult cos like I can’t say to him…”).

These items together point to **spontaneous, casual conversation**, not planned or formal discourse.

### 2.2. Stance and interpersonal work

- **sorry**: frequent in spoken interaction as a **stance and face‑work marker**:
  - apologising,
  - softening complaints,
  - managing potential offence (“I’m sorry if you don’t fit into it”, “I’m so sorry for this”).
- **guy**: informal person reference, often in gendered or relational talk (“from the guys”, “any guy that I’m with”).

The co‑occurrence of **expletives + stance markers (sorry, like) + informal person reference (guy)** suggests a dimension of **strongly evaluative, interpersonal talk**, where speakers are:
- expressing frustration, anger, or incredulity,
- positioning themselves vis‑à‑vis others (partners, colleagues, “guys”, “kids”),
- doing face‑work around potentially offensive content.

So, from the loadings alone, this pole looks like a **highly colloquial, emotionally expressive, sweary stance style**.

## 3. Evidence from high‑scoring examples

All ten examples are human turns with high f4_pos scores. They consistently show:

- heavy use of **fuck, shit, god, gonna, like, sorry**,
- narratives of **frustration, complaint, or strong evaluation**,
- intimate or interpersonal topics (relationships, work, neighbours, social life).

### 3.1. Expletives as evaluative and intensifying devices

Across excerpts, expletives are doing systematic discourse work:

- **Intensifying negative evaluation / complaint**
  - “it sounded horrendous it sounded really like too fucking much in many ways” (t000194)
  - “I've been back and told him that that shit is dangerous” (t001017)
  - “I had a really shit journey into work” (t000147)
  - “they are rough as fuck” (t000473)
  - “this kid is fucked in the head genuinely genuinely fucked in the head” (t001017)

- **Expressing frustration or anger**
  - “what the fuck is the matter with this kid?” (t001017)
  - “I'm so pissed off for trying it” (t001017)
  - “you're already having a fucking shit and they're like oh you owe us time great” (t000147)
  - “I didn't fucking help myself when I put that Facebook status” (t001077)

- **Reaction markers / narrative climaxes**
  - “oh shit I've done that before” (t001028)
  - “I thought oh thank god only five minutes to the bus … then I realised fuck it's Saturday” (t001028)
  - “I was like oh my god like you look horrendous” (t000473)
  - “and then you hear and you're like what the fuck” (t000473)

These uses show that the factor is not just “contains swear words”; it is **a discourse style where expletives are central resources for stance, evaluation, and narrative punchlines**.

### 3.2. Informal, conversational scaffolding: like, gonna, cos

The same texts are saturated with **like, gonna, cos** in classic conversational functions:

- **like** as discourse marker and quotative:
  - “like it's so frustrating the worst it's the worst” (t000147)
  - “I'm not one of these girls that like where the fuck were you?” (t001663)
  - “I was like oh thank god only five minutes to the bus” (t001028)
  - “I was like holy fuck bleach blond hair like breaking off cos it's that shit” (t000473)

- **gonna** as informal future:
  - “I'm gonna be my own fucking boss” (t000194)
  - “I'm not gonna sit around making him dinner” (t001017)
  - “you're not gonna have that after you've brushed your teeth” (t000702)
  - “they were always gonna do what they were gonna do” (t001077)

- **cos** (in the loadings, and present in the examples even when not highlighted):
  - “it's difficult cos like I can't say to him oh you're working too much” (t001663)
  - “it fucks me off cos I'm not his girlfriend” (t001017)

These features reinforce the interpretation of this pole as **highly colloquial, speech‑like, and interactional**, rather than informational or expository.

### 3.3. Interpersonal and affective content

The topics and narrative frames are consistently **interpersonal and affect‑heavy**:

- **Gendered power and workplace culture** (t000194):
  - “swing their dicks around suck my cock … I'm an alpha male you're not … shut up fuck off”
  - Strong stance against a male‑dominated, aggressive environment; expletives index both that culture and the speaker’s resistance.

- **Relationships and expectations** (t001663, t001181, t000978):
  - Negotiating partner’s work, jealousy norms, and self‑presentation (“I'm not one of these girls that like where the fuck were you?”).
  - Social awkwardness and disappointment (“we were just a bit like mm that sounds a bit shit like that's really awkward then”).

- **Social obligations and resentment** (t001017, t001077):
  - Anger at a neighbour and at being taken for granted (“this kid is fucked in the head”, “I'm not gonna sit around making him dinner”).
  - Reflection on online conflict and blame (“I were inviting this shit … we made it known”).

- **Everyday hassles and complaints** (t000147, t001028, t000473, t000702):
  - Commuting misery, cold weather, rough local norms, noisy sleepovers.

In all of these, expletives and informal markers are tightly bound up with **stance‑taking about people and situations**. The dimension is not just “swearing” but **swearing as a resource for affective, interpersonal positioning in everyday talk**.

### 3.4. Face‑work and mitigation: sorry

Where **sorry** appears, it often co‑occurs with or follows strong negative evaluation:

- “I'm so sorry for this I'm so pissed off for trying it” (t001017)
- “I'm sorry if you don't fit into it it's shit it's crap” (t000194)
- “can you be quieter? I'm sorry” (t000702)

This suggests a **tension between strong negative stance and politeness/face‑work**, typical of intimate conversation: speakers vent, swear, and then partially repair or soften.

## 4. Source scores: who drives this pole?

Mean f4 scores by group:

- human: **0.88**
- unprofiled_gpt: 0.18
- profiled_gpt: 0.10

Humans are **by far the main drivers** of this positive pole. Both GPT conditions are much closer to zero, indicating:

- LLM outputs, even when mimicking speakers, are **less likely to use heavy swearing and highly colloquial markers in the same density and configuration** as humans.
- The dimension therefore reflects a **distinctively human conversational style** in this corpus: spontaneous, emotionally intense, and unconstrained by moderation norms.

The fact that **profiled vs unprofiled GPT** are very similar on this factor suggests that **providing socio‑demographic profiles does not substantially push the model into this expletive‑rich style**; the main contrast is human vs LLM.

---

## 5. Consolidated interpretation

Putting the loadings, examples, and source scores together:

- Lexically, the pole is defined by **expletives (shit, fuck, god)** plus **informal discourse markers and future forms (like, cos, gonna)** and **stance/face‑work items (sorry, guy)**.
- Functionally, these items are used to:
  - express **strong affect** (anger, frustration, incredulity, amusement),
  - perform **intensified evaluation** of people and situations,
  - narrate **personal, interpersonal experiences**,
  - manage **face and social alignment** in the midst of potentially offensive talk.
- Socially, the style is **unselfconsciously informal and intimate**, characteristic of **peer‑to‑peer conversation** rather than institutional or carefully monitored speech.
- Distributionally, it is **strongly human‑driven**, with LLM outputs under both prompts under‑representing this style.

Hence, a suitable discourse‑dimension label for f4 (positive pole) is:

> **Expletive‑laden colloquial stance**  
> i.e. a human‑driven conversational style marked by dense swearing, informal markers, and strong affective and interpersonal positioning.
