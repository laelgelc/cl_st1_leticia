## 2. What do the high‑scoring excerpts actually do?

Across the 10 excerpts, the same discourse pattern recurs. Key features:

### 2.1. Narrative of problematic or frustrating events

Each excerpt recounts a **specific episode where things go wrong or are mishandled**:

- Snow and transport chaos; people “can’t be bothered” to go to work; authorities “didn’t bother” to grit (t002139).
- Unwanted sexual advance and awkward social dynamics in a night out (t002390).
- Administrative incompetence at a muddy cross‑country event (t002892).
- Minecraft building project ruined by a “bloke” misusing TNT (t002362).
- Riot police turning up “out of nowhere” at a student party (t002755).
- Football team with a “terrifying” keeper who “completely misses it” and a misused centre back (t002685).
- Typhoon disrupting first day at a new school; no taxis, soaked walk, lack of support (t002288).
- Ex‑employee “completely useless” who keeps turning up and trying to sue (t003013).
- Business idea abandoned because he “couldn’t be bothered” (t001989).
- Lost phone saga with no resolution yet (t002538).

These are not neutral stories; they are **mini‑complaints or accounts of hassle, incompetence, or unfairness**.

### 2.2. Intensified, polarised evaluation

The loading intensifiers and quantifiers are heavily used in context:

- “absolutely no sign that anyone had taken it seriously”  
- “completely stranded”, “completely ruined”, “completely drenched”  
- “nothing was done”, “no grit, no proper preparation, nothing moving”  
- “either nothing or everything with them”  
- “properly awkward”, “proper five‑man midfield”, “proper little business”  
- “totally vanished”, “absolutely useless”

The **nothing/everything** pattern is especially salient (t002755, t002288, t003013, t002538): it encodes **all‑or‑nothing, extreme framing** of events and people.

### 2.3. Temporal and event‑boundary marking

Narratives are structured with clear sequencing:

- “then there was that time years back…”, “on the one hand… on the other hand…” (t002139)
- “Then at one point… Later on… And then, out of nowhere…” (t002390)
- “Eventually… so I think… We head back later on… Then it turns out…” (t002892)
- “so after that disaster… afterwards…” (t002362)
- “nothing happened at all for ages… Then about an hour later… suddenly turned up” (t002755)

Words like **suddenly, later, afterwards** mark **turning points** and **escalations**, often into something worse or more awkward.

### 2.4. First‑person stance and mental commentary

Speakers foreground their own reactions and internal states:

- “what gets me is…”, “That really annoyed me” (t002139)
- “I wasn’t having that”, “it was just really blatant and made the whole thing feel even more awkward” (t002390)
- “I’m thinking, we’ve already done this” (t002892)
- “I remember just thinking, this has got to be some kind of joke” (t002288)
- “I’m just stood there thinking what’s the point?” (t002685)
- “I was already really wound up” (t002288)

This is **highly subjective, evaluative narration**, not just recounting events but **commenting on them as unfair, ridiculous, or exasperating**.

### 2.5. Colloquial, conversational register

The excerpts are full of informal markers:

- “yeah so we were out this one night”, “like really ambitious”, “loads of stupid shit”, “such a faff”
- “this bloke”, “this one lad”, “our keeper right he looks terrifying like properly scary”
- Frequent **just**, **like**, **yeah**, **you know**, and bare clause coordination.

This aligns with the loadings (bloke, alright, proper, just) and confirms that the dimension is about a **particular conversational storytelling style**, not formal exposition.

## 3. Which group drives this pole?

- Mean scores show **GPT (both profiled and unprofiled) are more extreme** on this pole than humans.
- All 10 top excerpts are **profiled_gpt**.
- The style itself (colloquial complaint‑narrative) is clearly human‑like, but GPT **over‑produces and intensifies** it when reconstructing turns, especially with socio‑demographic profiles that encourage regionally marked items (bloke, proper) and strong stance.

So this pole is **LLM‑amplified conversational grievance storytelling**, grounded in human conversational norms.

---

## 4. Interpreting the discourse dimension

Bringing the evidence together:

- **Functionally**: narrating problematic, frustrating, or unfair experiences; often mini‑rants or gripe stories.
- **Attitudinally**: strongly evaluative, often negative; speakers highlight incompetence, lack of effort, or bad luck.
- **Structurally**: temporally sequenced episodes with marked turning points (suddenly, later, afterwards).
- **Lexically**: heavy use of intensifiers (absolutely, totally, completely, proper), polar quantifiers (nothing, everything, anyone, even), and stance markers (bother, awful, expect, honestly, clearly).
- **Register**: informal, colloquial, often British English, with “bloke”, “proper”, “alright”, “yeah so…”.

This is not just “narrative” in general; it is **narrative plus evaluative complaint**, with a strong sense of **things going wrong and people not doing what they should**.

## 5. Possible labels for this pole

Here are several candidate labels that capture different facets of the same underlying dimension:

1. **Intensified Colloquial Complaint Narration**  
   - Highlights: colloquial register + narrative structure + strong negative evaluation.

2. **Everyday Grievance Storytelling**  
   - Emphasises the mundane, day‑to‑day nature of the episodes and their function as gripe stories.

3. **Subjective Hassle Narratives**  
   - Focuses on the speaker’s subjective stance and the framing of events as hassles or farces.

4. **All‑or‑Nothing Evaluative Narratives**  
   - Draws on the nothing/everything, absolutely/completely pattern of polarised evaluation.

5. **Colloquial Problem‑Focused Stance**  
   - A slightly more abstract label foregrounding stance and problem orientation.

If I had to choose one primary label for this pole, balancing precision and generality, I would propose:

> **“Intensified Everyday Grievance Narration”**

- **Intensified**: captures absolutely, totally, completely, proper, massive, awful.
- **Everyday**: these are ordinary life events (snow, parties, school, football, work, phones).
- **Grievance**: encodes the complaint/annoyance orientation (bother, awful, couldn’t be bothered, useless, farce).
- **Narration**: reflects the clear temporal sequencing and story‑like structure (later, suddenly, afterwards, stand, carry, stick).

This label fits both the **lexical profile** (loadings) and the **discourse behaviour** in the excerpts, and it aligns with the fact that GPT, especially under profiled prompts, is the main driver of this pole by elaborating human‑style complaint stories into highly intensified, colloquial narratives.
