This positive pole of Factor 2 is best understood as a discourse of *conceptualised processes and constructed structures*—speakers are explicitly talking about how things are built up, become something, and differ from alternatives, often in a fairly reflective, meta-organisational way.

Below I propose labels and justify them from (1) the loadings and (2) the excerpts, then (3) identify which group drives the pole.

## 1. What the loadings tell us

High-loading items (with approximate glosses):

- **build (.63)** – lexically encodes *construction over time* (build relationships, build case studies, build tunnels, build a system).
- **themselves (.62)** – reflexive focus on agents as *participants in their own construction* (they themselves appear; let them do it themselves).
- **become (.57)** – change-of-state, *process of turning into* something (become “their” careers person; become a little team).
- **different (.44)** – explicit *contrast* and differentiation (different institutions, different angles, different scale).
- **sense (.44)** – *conceptual grasp* or understanding (sense of how a service could be organised; sense of a big picture; sense of what freedom ought to look like).
- **fact (.43)** – metadiscursive, marking *propositional status* (the fact that…, after the fact).
- **rather (.43) / than (.43)** – comparative framing, *choosing one configuration over another* (rather than just chatting; rather than treating relationships as mysterious).
- **set (.37)** – *configurations or frameworks* (set‑up, set of videos, set them up).
- **far (.36), more (.36), almost (.32)** – scalar and approximative *gradation* (far more chance, far more value, almost everyone).
- **real (.36)** – *authenticity / actuality* (real-life case studies, real upheaval, real shame).
- **also (.34)** – additive linking, building *cumulative arguments*.
- **own (.33)** – *ownership / individuation* (my own experience, their own digital CVs, their own imagination).
- **idea (.31)** – explicit *conceptualisation* (initial idea, whole idea, powerful idea).
- **lot (.31)** – quantity, *extent* (a lot of what you’ve been saying, a lot of retreats, a lot of the heavy lifting).

Taken together, these loadings point to a discourse that:

- Focuses on **processes of change and development** (build, become).
- Emphasises **structured configurations** (set, set‑up, set of videos, portfolios, programmes).
- Uses **comparative and contrastive framing** (different, rather than, far more).
- Is **meta-conceptual**: speakers talk about *ideas, sense, fact*—how things are understood, organised, or should be.
- Highlights **agency and self-construction** (themselves, own).
- Uses **scalar and quantifying language** to evaluate scope and impact (far, more, almost, lot).

So at the level of the lexicon, this pole is not about a topic like “education” or “politics” per se, but about a *way of talking*: reflective, comparative, process-oriented, and concerned with how social/organisational realities are built and structured.

## 2. What the excerpts show

All ten high-scoring texts are profiled GPT turns, and they consistently instantiate the pattern suggested by the loadings.

Several excerpts describe **institutional or organisational structures being built up over time**:

- Excerpt 001: careers service work:

  This is a textbook example of *process + structure*: building relationships, portfolios, strands, programmes, and adjusting them over time.

- Excerpt 006: corporate education liaison:

  Again, explicit talk about *constructing a role and programme*, joining dots, defining concepts.

- Excerpt 010: retreats:

  Here the discourse is about *growth and scaling*—how a small initiative becomes a larger structure.

### 2.2. Conceptual design and meta-level “idea talk”

Several excerpts are explicitly about **ideas for projects or pedagogical designs**, not just recounting events:

- Excerpt 003: case-study project:

  This is highly *meta*: the speaker is designing a conceptual and material structure (a case-study format), contrasting it with alternatives (“rather than just chatting”, “rather than just free content”).

- Excerpt 004: chain of interviews:
  - “the chain builds quite naturally”
  - “By the time you’ve done all five, the set of videos themselves becomes a kind of narrative”
  - “each interview … pushes the story forward, adds a bit more to that picture of what connects them all”

  The speaker is conceptualising a **narrative structure** emerging from linked interviews.

- Excerpt 005: investor-facing project:
  - “what’s really powerful about this idea is that it shows investors a clear journey”
  - “here’s the raw starting point, and here’s how it can be developed and improved”
  - “If we set it up so people can log in … they themselves appear … for their own digital CVs”
  - “It becomes evidence of their own articulated responses”

  Again, explicit design thinking: how to set something up, what its value depends on, how it functions as evidence.

- Excerpt 009: mapping relationships:
  - “the whole idea is really about breaking relationships down into explicit, visible steps, rather than treating them as this mysterious thing”
  - “By mapping that out, you’re showing them: here’s how that meeting could happen, here are the links in the chain.”
  - “relationships are built piece by piece”

  This is a *meta-model* of social relationships, made explicit and stepwise.

### 2.3. Comparative, contrastive, and evaluative framing

The high-loading items **different, rather, than, far, more, real, almost** appear in contexts where speakers:

- Contrast **pedagogical approaches** (Excerpt 002):
  - “really big, knotty historical topics … far more chance of actually catching their curiosity”
  - “so many different angles”
  - “My own experience … was nothing like that”

- Contrast **freedom and control across contexts** (Excerpt 008):

  - “somewhere like China … on a completely different scale”
  - “As more people see what life is like elsewhere, and as they become more aware, they begin to want more say”

- Contrast **adult behaviour with children** (Excerpt 007):

  - “I set them up … and then let them carry on”

  - “If you’re constantly doing everything with children, they never get the chance to use their own imagination”
  - “You give them a few ideas … and then you should step back and let them do it themselves.”

Across these texts, the discourse is not just descriptive; it is **analytical and contrastive**, often implicitly normative: this way of structuring things is better / more effective than that way.

### 2.4. Agency, self-construction, and reflexivity

The high loading of **themselves, own, become** is reflected in:

- People as **agents in their own development**:
  - “they themselves appear … for their own digital CVs” (Excerpt 005)

This reinforces the idea that the discourse is about **how people and structures mutually constitute each other over time**.

## 3. Which group drives this pole?

Mean factor scores:

- Human: 0.24

All three groups are on the positive side, but **both GPT conditions score substantially higher than humans**, with **profiled GPT highest**. All ten top-scoring excerpts are from **profiled_gpt**.

So:

- This pole is **strongly driven by LLM-generated turns**, especially when the model is given a socio-demographic profile.
- The style is recognisably **LLM-like**: elaborated, meta-structural, with explicit talk of “ideas”, “set‑ups”, “programmes”, “structures”, and “journeys”, and frequent comparative framing (“rather than”, “far more”).

## 4. Proposed labels for this positive pole

Any label should capture:

- Process and change (build, become).
- Structuring and configuration (set, set‑up, portfolios, programmes, chains).
- Conceptual / meta-discursive stance (idea, sense, fact).
- Contrastive, evaluative framing (different, rather than, far more, real).
- Agency and self-construction (themselves, own).

Possible labels:

1. **“Constructed Processes and Organised Structures”**

   - “Constructed” reflects *build, become, themselves, own*.
   - “Processes” captures the temporal, developmental aspect.
   - “Organised Structures” reflects *set, set‑up, programme, portfolio, chain, narrative*.

2. **“Meta-Organisational and Conceptualising Discourse”**

   - “Meta-Organisational” for talk about how services, roles, programmes, and relationships are *organised and coordinated*.
   - “Conceptualising” for the explicit focus on *ideas, sense, fact*, and on designing or rethinking systems.

3. **“Reflective Design and Structuring Talk”**

   - “Reflective” for the evaluative, contrastive stance (different, rather than, far more, real shame).
   - “Design and Structuring” for the recurrent pattern of *proposing, mapping, and building* structures (case studies, chains of interviews, liaison roles, relationship maps).

---

## 5. Concise characterisation

In functional terms, this positive pole represents a **discourse of reflective, design-oriented structuring of social and institutional processes**. Speakers (here, predominantly GPT under profiled prompting) talk about:

- How things are or should be **built up over time**.
- How roles, programmes, and relationships are **configured and coordinated**.
- How different options **compare**, and why one structure is preferable “rather than” another.
- How individuals and groups **become** what they are through these processes, often doing things **themselves** and for their **own** purposes.

Any of the labels above would be defensible; if a single label is needed, I would choose:

> **Constructed Processes and Organised Structures**
