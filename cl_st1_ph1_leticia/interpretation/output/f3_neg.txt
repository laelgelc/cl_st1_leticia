Interpretation of Factor f3 (negative pole)

1. Which group drives this pole?

- Mean scores:
  - Human: 3.38
  - Profiled GPT: 1.41
  - Unprofiled GPT: 1.39  

Because we are interpreting the *negative* pole, we look for the *lowest* mean scores. Both GPT conditions cluster low and very close together; humans are much higher. So:

- The negative pole is strongly driven by **LLM‑generated turns**, especially **unprofiled GPT** (all 10 high‑scoring examples are unprofiled GPT).
- Human turns are comparatively *distant* from this pole.

So this dimension’s negative pole is characterising a style that is particularly typical of GPT outputs, not of human speakers.

2. Linguistic profile of the negative pole

Loadings on f3_neg:

- huge (-0.70)
- (em (-0.50))  [likely a filled pause / discourse marker, but not visible in the examples]
- massive (-0.44)
- finally (-0.39)
- (awful (-0.35))

Core lexical pattern:

- **Size/intensity boosters**: huge, massive
- **Temporal culmination marker**: finally
- **Evaluative negative adjective**: awful
- Plus a probable spoken‑style hesitation marker (em).

These are all **stance‑laden, colloquial, high‑impact items**. They do not describe specific domains (e.g. politics, technology) but rather a way of *framing* events and experiences: as big, intense, culminating, or strongly evaluated.

3. What the examples show

All examples are conversational narratives or complaints, with a strong sense of:

- **Scale / magnitude** (huge, massive)
- **Build‑up and release over time** (finally)
- **Affective evaluation** (frustration, stress, jealousy, resentment)

a. “huge / massive” as scalar and affective amplifiers

Across the excerpts, huge/massive are used to:

- Quantify **large amounts**:
  - “these huge amounts of food”
  - “a massive amount of administration”
  - “a huge weekly wage”
  - “this massive workload”
- Emphasise **burden / responsibility / impact**:
  - “we’re still the ones who carry most of the responsibility”
  - “this massive deadline looming”
  - “such a massive impact on everything”
- Characterise **psychological states**:
  - “this massive seething ball of nerves and neurosis”

These are not neutral measurements; they are **subjective amplifiers** that dramatise the situation. The speaker is positioning events as overwhelming, excessive, or impressive.

b. “finally” as narrative culmination

In the “finally” excerpts, the word marks:

- The **end of a long‑running desire or delay**:
  - “So I finally got to go to this place I’d been wanting to go to all summer”
- The **end of a difficult process / journey**:
  - “once that second bus finally turned up”
  - “by the time I finally walked in the door”

“Finally” here is a **discourse‑organising device**: it signals the culmination of a narrative sequence and often coincides with emotional payoff (relief, frustration, resentment).

c. Underlying discourse functions

Across the examples, the negative pole is associated with:

- **Narrative elaboration of personal experience**:
  - Work stress, travel hardship, family conflict, jealousy about body/food, financial fantasies.
- **Subjective stance and evaluation**:
  - Strong feelings: jealousy, anger, frustration, exhaustion, resentment.
- **Dramatisation through scalar and temporal framing**:
  - Big quantities, big burdens, big impacts, long waits, eventual outcomes.

The language is **colloquial, affect‑rich, and story‑like**, with a clear sense of the speaker “telling you what it was like” and how big/long/intense it felt.

4. Integrating loadings and examples

Giving equal weight to loadings and examples:

- The loadings point to a **lexical cluster of intensification and culmination** (huge, massive, finally, awful, em).
- The examples show these items embedded in **first‑person, emotionally coloured narratives** about everyday life, often with a complaining or venting tone.

This suggests that the negative pole is not about a specific topic, but about a **discourse mode**:

- **Experiential, narrative, and evaluative**, foregrounding:
  - The *size* or *weight* of problems, tasks, or experiences.
  - The *duration* and *eventual resolution* of difficult situations.
  - The speaker’s *emotional stance* toward these experiences.

5. Possible labels for this pole

Any label should capture:

- Scalar amplification (huge, massive)
- Temporal culmination (finally)
- Evaluative / affective colouring (awful, plus the complaint/venting content)
- Conversational narrative framing

Plausible labels:

1. **“Intensified Personal Experience Narration”**  
   - Highlights that the discourse is narrative (“experience narration”) and that it is linguistically boosted (“intensified”) via huge, massive, finally, awful.

2. **“Dramatised Everyday Complaint Discourse”**  
   - Captures the recurrent complaining/venting function and the dramatising role of the scalar and temporal markers.

3. **“Colloquial Stance‑Intensifying Narrative Style”**  
   - Emphasises the colloquial register and the stance‑intensifying function of the key loadings.

Given the evidence, the most precise and portable label for this pole is:

> **Intensified Personal Experience Narration**

This label reflects:

- The **narrative, first‑person, experiential** nature of the excerpts.
- The **lexical intensification** of scale and culmination (huge, massive, finally, awful).
- The fact that this style is particularly characteristic of **LLM‑generated conversational turns**, especially under the unprofiled prompt condition.
