This factor pole is best interpreted as a **“quantified, time‑ and money‑anchored narration”** dimension, strongly associated with **human** speakers.

---

## 1. Who drives this pole?

Mean scores:

- Human: **0.56**
- Profiled GPT: **0.34**
- Unprofiled GPT: **0.32**

Humans are clearly the main drivers of this pole; both GPT conditions pattern similarly and lower. So the discourse type here is characteristically human in this corpus.

---

## 2. Linguistic profile of the pole

High positive loadings:

- Numbers: **pound (.83), four (.77), hundred (.58), six (.57), three (.51), ten (.48), two (.42), five (.36), single (.32), minute (.30)**
- Time/money unit: **month (.55)**
- Verb: **manage (.31)**

Key properties:

1. **Dense use of specific numerals** (especially low integers: two, three, four, five, six, ten, hundred).
2. **Monetary reference**: pound, hundred, often in combination (e.g. “two hundred pound”, “six hundred and thirty-two pounds”).
3. **Temporal reference**: month, minute, and implicit time spans (hours, days, years) in the examples.
4. **Quantification of events and resources**: money, time, frequency, duration, quantities of objects (pipes, wagons, notes, visits).
5. **‘Manage’** suggests talk about coping with or handling these quantified constraints (though it doesn’t appear in the top 10 examples, its loading fits the pattern of resource management).

This is not just “numbers in general”; it is **numbers as practical measures of time, money, and countable events**.

---

## 3. What the examples show

Across the high‑scoring excerpts, the loading items are used to:

### a. Talk about money and value

- Wages and hourly rates:
  - “I was getting **two hundred pounds** … it worked out I was being paid **a pound an hour**” (t001145)
- Bills, insurance, and compensation:
  - “six hundred and thirty-**two pounds** … last year … **four hundred and eighty pounds** … over a **hundred and fifty pounds**” (t000100)
  - “they would have rented out Mexborough for **eight hundred pound a month** gave them **two or three grand** plus compensation” (t001668)
- Prices and savings:
  - “for **nine ninety-nine a month** you get everything … that comes to erm that’s a **hundred two hundred pound**” (t000849)
  - “if the car’s worth **six hundred quid** now I don’t wanna keep spending **two or three hundred pound** at a time to fix it” (t001202)

Here, numbers are used to **evaluate, compare, and justify** financial decisions.

### b. Talk about time and duration

- Months and years:
  - “this is **four years** ago … I’ve gone travelling for **three months**” (t000367)
  - “every **three months** you’ve got to do him” (t000849)
- Hours and days (even when not loading words, they co‑occur with the loaded numerals):
  - “we were working **ten hours a day five days a week** … **fifty hours a week**” (t001145)
  - “I would leave at **five** and would get there for **seven** … got to half past **ten** … it only took me **two hours** to get home” (t000434)
- Minutes:
  - “each group will have **ten to fifteen minutes** to interview the guest” (t001151)

Numbers here anchor **narratives in time**, giving precise durations and schedules.

### c. Count and quantify events, objects, and people

- Game money and counting:
  - “a **five pound** note … same as **five pounds** … one **two three four five six seven eight nine ten**” (t001480)
- Workloads and logistics:
  - “three or **four wagons** of pipe arriving a day … up to **six** arriving a day with **four pipes** on each that’s **twenty-four pipes** maximum” (t000164)
- People and visits:
  - “there’s **two** others so they’ve got **four vets**” (t000849)
  - “he came up to Cambridge like **five times**” (t000367)
- Programme days:
  - “day **two** … day **three** … day **four** … day **five** … day **six**” (t001151)

Numbers are used to **structure accounts**: how many, how often, in what sequence.

---

## 4. Discourse function

Taken together, the loadings and examples point to a discourse style where speakers:

- **Narrate personal experiences** (jobs, travel, relationships, weather events, games, logistics).
- **Anchor those narratives in concrete, quantifiable details** of:
  - money (wages, bills, compensation, prices),
  - time (months, hours, minutes, years),
  - counts (people, items, journeys, days in a programme).

The numbers are not decorative; they are **doing discourse work**:

- Justifying stances (“I’m not paying it”, “that’s jus ridiculous”).
- Evaluating fairness or value (“I was being paid a pound an hour”, “far cheaper”).
- Demonstrating effort or commitment (“I’ve driven a hundred miles and I’ve got there”).
- Organising complex sequences (“day two … day three … day four … day five … day six”).

This is a **resource‑accounting, experience‑anchoring** way of talking: speakers make their stories credible, vivid, and evaluable by tying them to specific quantities.

---

## 5. Possible labels for this pole

Any label should capture:

- Heavy **lexical quantification**,
- Focus on **money and time**,
- Use in **narrative and evaluative talk**,
- Its being **human‑driven** in this corpus.

Plausible labels:

1. **Quantified Personal Accounting**
   - Emphasises the use of numbers to “account for” experiences, especially in financial and temporal terms.

2. **Time‑ and Money‑Anchored Narration**
   - Highlights that narratives are anchored in concrete measures of time and money.

3. **Everyday Resource Quantification**
   - Focuses on everyday resources (hours, months, pounds, items) being quantified and evaluated.

4. **Numerically Detailed Experience Talk**
   - Stresses the numerically fine‑grained nature of personal experience narratives.

Any of these would be defensible; if a single label is needed, **“Quantified Personal Accounting”** best captures both the lexical pattern (numbers, pound, month) and the discourse function (explaining, justifying, and evaluating lived experiences through precise quantities), with humans as the primary users of this style in the corpus.
