Factor 6 (positive pole) is a very “thin” factor lexically – only three items load, and two of them strongly:

- em (1.32)
- alright (.76)
- odd (.39)

The mean source scores show that:

- Humans: ≈ 0.00 (slightly positive)
- Profiled GPT: ≈ -0.32
- Unprofiled GPT: ≈ -0.31

So this positive pole is clearly human‑driven; both GPT conditions sit well into the negative side of the factor.

Because the loadings are so concentrated, the interpretation has to lean heavily on what these items *do* in interaction, and how they pattern in the high‑scoring texts.

---

## What the loadings tell us

### 1. em

- Very high loading (1.32), and in the excerpts it appears repeatedly:
  - “em I really wanted to wait…”
  - “em well you know if you flaunt it…”
  - “oh okay I’ll em I’ll speak to her…”
  - “he bought em as we were leaving the bar…”
- Functionally, this is a classic filled pause / hesitation marker in spontaneous speech.
- It signals:
  - On‑line planning and formulation
  - Turn‑holding (keeping the floor while thinking)
  - Informal, unmonitored, “live” production

### 2. alright

- Moderately strong loading (.76), and very frequent in the examples:
  - Turn‑openers / acknowledgements: “hi Alright well that’s fine…”
  - Reassurance / mitigation: “it’s alright for any man to just come and take her…”, “no it’s alright come to my rescue…”
  - Evaluations: “he’d been alright when I was chucking up…”, “they’re alright like at Fords…”
  - Formulaic social routines: “are you alright?”, “oh I’m alright she said…”
- Pragmatically, alright is doing:
  - Discourse management (“alright well that’s fine…”)
  - Stance‑softening and reassurance (“it’s alright…”)
  - Relational work in greetings and small talk (“are you alright?”)

### 3. odd

- Weak loading (.39) and not visible in the top 10 excerpts.
- Likely contributes a minor nuance of evaluative stance (“it’s odd that…”) but is not central to the dimension.

Taken together, the loadings point to a factor defined by:

- High density of *spontaneous‑speech disfluency* (em)
- High density of *interactional, stance‑softening discourse markers* (alright)
- Mild evaluative stance (odd)

## What the excerpts show

All ten high‑scoring texts are human turns. They are:

- Highly disfluent, with repetitions, false starts, and “em/erm/eh”:
  - “I I went back on recently…”
  - “there was such misogyny there was this idea that em well you know…”
  - “I I whacked him a few times…”
- Strongly conversational in structure:
  - Embedded reported dialogue: “oh are you alright how you doing?”, “oh hello how are you? as you do”, “oh you alright I’m nice to meet you”
  - Phone‑call style organisation: “hi Alright well that’s fine I’ve left it out in the hall for you…”
- Rich in interpersonal work:
  - Reassuring / mitigating: “that’s fine…”, “it’s alright…”, “they’re alright like at Fords…”
  - Empathy and concern: “oh I’m alright she said…”, “you don’t like to come and knock on the door do you…”
  - Social evaluation and stance: “that’s absolutely ridiculous”, “there was such misogyny…”, “I wouldn’t take any crap…”

Crucially, the loading words are doing *interactional* rather than propositional work:

- em keeps the floor and signals thinking aloud.
- alright manages the relationship and the flow of talk (greetings, closings, reassurance, acceptance, mild evaluation).

This is exactly the kind of micro‑interactional texture that LLM outputs typically under‑produce: GPT responses, even when “conversational”, are much more fluent, with fewer filled pauses and fewer colloquial discourse markers like alright in these specific functions.

---

## Who drives the pole?

- Humans cluster around zero but are the only group with many *high* positive scores.
- Both GPT conditions are clearly negative on this factor.
- So the positive pole is a *human‑speech interactional style* that GPT does not reproduce, even when profiled.

This suggests the factor is not about topic or content, but about *mode of production and interactional texture*:

- On‑line, disfluent, co‑present talk vs. planned, fluent, “typed” style.
- The positive pole is the “live, messy, interpersonal” end of that continuum.

---

## Discourse interpretation of f6 (positive pole)

Focusing only on this pole, and giving equal weight to loadings and examples, the dimension encoded here is:

- A style of talk that is:
  - Spontaneous and unplanned (heavy use of em)
  - Strongly interaction‑oriented (alright as greeting, reassurance, acceptance)
  - Interpersonally engaged, with lots of stance‑softening and relational work

It is *not* primarily about narrative vs. argument, or formal vs. informal content; it cuts across topics (Minecraft, misogyny, plumbing mishaps, holidays, illness, relationships). What unifies the texts is the *interactional management of talk* in real time.

### Plausible labels for f6 (positive pole)

Any of the following would capture the discourse encoded at this pole:

1. **Spontaneous, interaction‑managed conversation**
   - Emphasises on‑line production (em) and discourse management (alright).

2. **Hesitant, floor‑holding conversational style**
   - Highlights the role of em in holding the floor and signalling planning.

3. **Interpersonal reassurance and small‑talk discourse**
   - Draws out alright’s role in greetings, “I’m alright” responses, and reassurance.

4. **Live, disfluent, relational talk**

   - Captures both the disfluency and the relational/interpersonal orientation.

If I had to choose a single label that best integrates the loadings and the examples, I would propose:

> **“Spontaneous, disfluent, interaction‑oriented conversation”**

- *Spontaneous, disfluent* → anchored in em and the evident on‑line planning.
- *Interaction‑oriented* → anchored in alright and the dense interpersonal work in the excerpts.
- *Conversation* → reflects that this is a human‑driven, spoken‑style pole, distinct from the more fluent, text‑like GPT turns.
