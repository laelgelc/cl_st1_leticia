Interpretation of Factor f4 (negative pole)

1. Which group drives this pole?

- Mean scores:
  - Human: 0.88  
  - Unprofiled GPT: 0.18  
  - Profiled GPT: 0.10  

The factor is named “f4_neg”, but numerically humans are strongly positive on f4, while both GPT conditions are close to zero. The high‑scoring texts at the *negative* pole are all from profiled_gpt. So:

- The *dimension* itself is anchored in human language use (humans are far from zero).
- The *negative pole* specifically is exemplified and driven by profiled GPT outputs (the most negative scores we see are all profiled_gpt).

So this pole characterises a discourse style that GPT (especially with profiling) tends to produce more strongly than humans, relative to the rest of the corpus.

2. Lexical loadings and what they suggest

Negative loadings (strongest first):

- odd (−.73)
- sense (−.41)
- plan (−.37)
- properly (−.36)
- chat (−.35)
- once (−.34)
- straight (−.33)
- build (−.31)
- change (−.30)

These cluster semantically into:

a) Planning / sequencing / procedure
- plan, once, straight, build, change, properly  
  → talk about steps, ordering, getting things “straight”, making or revising a plan, doing things “properly”, “building” something up.

b) Evaluation of normality / appropriateness
- odd, sense, properly, straight  
  → metacommentary on whether things are normal, sensible, done correctly, or “odd”.

c) Interpersonal talk
- chat, chat(ting)  
  → explicit reference to conversational episodes (“had a proper little chat”, “carried on chatting”).

Overall, the loadings point to discourse that:

- narrates or explains *processes and plans* in a stepwise way,
- includes *meta‑evaluation* of how things are done (properly / straight / odd / makes sense),
- embeds this in *reported interaction* or “chat”.

3. What the examples show

Across the 10 excerpts, the same discourse pattern recurs. Key features:

a) Stepwise, procedural or project‑oriented narration

- Ex. 001: organising a trip; sequence of tasks:
  - “First I need to phone… Once I’ve got that straight, I’ll email her properly, and then this evening I’ll ring everyone…”
- Ex. 003: designing an exhibition:
  - “For the moment I’m just working on the plan on the computer, trying to get this up properly, maybe change some of the titles, and then open it all in Illustrator…”
- Ex. 004: life‑course planning:
  - “I’m planning to do a paid internship… Once I’ve done that… After the travelling, I’ll decide whether to go straight into a job or…”
- Ex. 006: car bodywork repair:
  - “He’ll start off by making a pattern… Then he gets his plasma cutter out… clamps a nice straight bit of metal… Once that’s gone, he uses the card pattern to cut a new piece…”

These are not just narratives; they are *proceduralised* narratives: clear temporal and logical sequencing, with an emphasis on doing things in the right order and in the right way.

b) Concern with correctness, precision, and doing things “properly”

- “email her properly”
- “get this up properly, maybe change some of the titles”
- “he doesn’t just bodge it, he does it properly”
- “nice straight bit of metal… perfectly straight edge”
- “only three of us can really read music properly”
- “trying to get it all straight in my head”
- “told her straight I was worried… she didn’t seem to be thinking straight”
- “once we’re absolutely sure what it is… we can add the details when we’ve got them properly”

This is a strong metadiscourse of *correctness, exactness, and clarity*—both in physical tasks and in thinking/talking.

c) Planning, revision, and contingency

- “just in case she has changed her mind”
- “everything’s a bit on hold in that sense”
- “maybe change some of the titles”
- “I’ll decide whether to go straight into a job or… I’m not totally sure yet”
- “the whole plan hasn’t quite worked out as I imagined”
- “working out the next steps and that”
- “no point in guessing and then having to redo it”

Speakers are explicitly *managing plans*, anticipating change, and reflecting on whether plans have worked.

d) Evaluation of normality / oddness / sense

- “as if nothing odd had happened at all”
- “it is slightly odd not to have any kind of letter explaining what they’ve done”
- “that actually makes sense from their point of view”
- “the whole business is just mental when you think about it”
- “she didn’t seem to be thinking straight”

This is evaluative commentary about whether events and practices are normal, sensible, or anomalous.

e) Embedded interpersonal “chat”

- “carried on chatting away with this bloke, going on and on”
- “I did have a proper little chat with her last night”
- “in with my tutor, chatting through what I’m doing and what the plan is”

The “chat” is often where plans are negotiated or concerns are expressed; it’s not small talk but *task‑ or problem‑oriented conversation*.

4. Discourse function of this pole

Balancing the loadings and examples, this pole encodes a discourse style that is:

- **Procedural and project‑focused**: narrating tasks, projects, and life plans as sequences of steps.
- **Meticulously evaluative**: frequent attention to whether things are done correctly, sensibly, or in an orderly way.
- **Reflective and corrective**: monitoring when plans don’t work, revising them, and commenting on “odd” or suboptimal practices.
- **Interpersonally mediated**: plans and concerns are often worked through in “chats” with others (tutors, family, staff).

It is not simply “narrative” or “instructional”; it is *reflective procedural talk*—people (or GPT imitating people) thinking through and talking through how to do things properly, how to get things straight, and how to adjust when reality is “odd” or plans fail.

5. Possible labels for this pole

Any label should capture:

- planning / sequencing,
- correctness / straightness / properness,
- reflective evaluation of sense/oddness,
- conversational negotiation of these issues.

Plausible labels:

1. **Reflective Planning and Procedure Talk**  
   - Emphasises the stepwise, plan‑oriented narration plus the reflective commentary on doing things properly and revising plans.

2. **Orderly, ‘Doing‑It‑Properly’ Discourse**  
   - Highlights the strong “properly / straight / sense / odd” evaluative frame around tasks and plans.

3. **Process‑Focused, Evaluative Chat**  
   - Brings in both the process orientation (plan, once, straight, build, change) and the interpersonal “chat” through which these processes are discussed and assessed.

Among these, for analytic clarity in LMDA terms, I would prioritise:

- **Reflective Planning and Procedure Talk**

because it best integrates:

- the *procedural/stepwise* nature of the discourse (plan, once, straight, build, change),
- the *reflective/evaluative* stance (odd, sense, properly, straight),
- and the conversational embedding (chat) without over‑narrowing to any single topic domain.

6. Role of source groups

- Humans overall are high on the *factor*, but the *negative pole* is most clearly realised in profiled GPT texts.
- This suggests that when GPT is given socio‑demographic profiles, it tends to produce especially *orderly, reflective, plan‑and‑procedure talk* of the kind captured at this pole—perhaps over‑systematising everyday narratives into well‑structured, “properly” sequenced accounts.

So, for this pole alone, a concise interpretation is:

> f4 (negative pole) encodes a discourse dimension of **Reflective Planning and Procedure Talk**: conversational accounts that carefully sequence actions and plans, evaluate whether things are done “properly” or “straight”, and comment on oddities or needed changes, often negotiated through “chat” with others.
